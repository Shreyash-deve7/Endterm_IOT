UDP is preferred over TCP for real-time video streaming because it is connectionless and does not wait for acknowledgements or retransmit lost packets. In live video, the most important thing is low latency, so old frames are useless if they arrive late. TCP would try to resend any lost packet and hold up the stream until it is delivered in order, which causes lag and buffering. UDP simply keeps sending the newest frames without any delay, which keeps the video smooth and real-time even if a few packets are lost along the way. When a UDP frame is dropped, the visual artefact observed is usually a momentary freeze, a flicker, or a partially decoded frame that may appear glitched, smeared, or with blocky distorted regions, before the next good frame arrives and the display recovers.
